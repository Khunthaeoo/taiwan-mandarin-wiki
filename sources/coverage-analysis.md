# Source coverage analysis — audited collection

**Audit date:** 2026-11-20  
**Scope:** Existing six *A Course in Contemporary Chinese* volumes (ACCC-01–06); six newly inspected audio folders (ACCC-07–12); two unique local PDFs (ACCC-13–14); one local video pending verification (ACCC-15); the MOE manual/EPUB (ACCC-16); NTU *華語正音* (ACCC-17); two Taiwan Mandarin Educational Resources Center resources (ACCC-18–19); and two NAER resources (ACCC-20–21).

## Inventory and source-family count

- **21 distinct works/material entries:** 6 textbooks + 6 audio-volume folders + 2 unique PDF works + 1 video + 6 official online resources. The MOE EPUB is a local copy of the same manual as its online HTML edition and is not counted twice. The two similarly named *Worshiping the Chinese God of Love* PDFs are one identical work.
- **7 identifiable independent source families:** (1) *A Course in Contemporary Chinese*; (2) Fulbright Taiwan Foundation / FLTA Story Book Series; (3) Innovative Language Learning / ChineseClass101; (4) Ministry of Education; (5) National Taiwan University; (6) FICHET / Taiwan Mandarin Educational Resources Center; (7) National Academy for Educational Research (NAER). The two FICHET products and two NAER resources are each grouped as one family. The six textbooks are one family, not six. The audio folders are tentatively linked to that series by names and lesson counts, but the audio itself does not verify the link. The video creator/source is unknown and is not counted as an identified family.
- **Reliability:** Six attributable families are institutional or otherwise traceable for language-learning use: ACCC, Fulbright/FLTA, MOE, NTU, FICHET, and NAER. ChineseClass101 is a traceable commercial supplement but not an academic authority. Audio attribution remains provisional; the video is not yet a usable or assessable source.

## Section and skill coverage

| Area | Assessment | Evidence and limitations |
|---|---|---|

The MOE manual is a reference, not a graduated exercise sequence; the NTU full course is paid. Video ACCC-15 is unverified and is not counted as evidence. |

| **Four tones and tone changes** | **Good introductory/reference evidence; advanced coverage incomplete** | MOE tone-mark reference and NTU CH5 cover tones/light tone and tone change; ACCC introductions and Survival Phrases lesson 2 (p. 11) offer additional examples. These sources do not yet establish a complete, levelled account of all tone-sandhi patterns or listening discrimination practice. |
| **Vocabulary A1–C1** | **Broad thematic material; level coverage unverified** | ACCC offers daily through advanced topic vocabulary, Huayu101 and Survival Phrases give practical phrases, ACCC-19 adds Taiwan-culture vocabulary, and NAER's first-level prototype (ACCC-21) directly shows 你好 and a name-related example. This provides a beginner reference, not a validated A1 inventory or project-level mapping; TBCL Level 1 is below CEFR A1 in several skills (ACCC-20). The series' collective CEFR A1–C1 statement also does not map individual lessons to this project's five levels. |

| **Speaking and real-life conversations** | **Good examples, incomplete progression/feedback** | ACCC dialogues and tasks, Huayu101 scenario phrases, FLTA reader dialogue, Survival Phrases, and ACCC-19 role-play support speaking. MOE's character-annotation reference is useful but not composition instruction. Beginner character writing, progressive writing tasks, and verified answer support remain gaps. |
| **Taiwan daily-life expressions** | **Good beginner scenarios; partial comprehensive coverage** | Huayu101 covers 12 scenarios; Survival Phrases supplies basic travel/service phrases; ACCC Vols. 1–4 have everyday dialogues. Phrases need review for current usage and appropriate register. |

Some material is topical or narrative rather than a neutral reference. Check contested, time-sensitive, Indigenous, religious, and public-policy claims against current primary sources; do not generalize one story to all Taiwanese people. |
| **Burmese explanations** | **Missing** | None of the inspected sources provides Burmese instructional explanations. This remains a core project gap requiring original translation and review by a qualified Burmese-speaking Mandarin educator. |

## Source differences and access notes

- ACCC's introductory material emphasizes Pinyin and discusses Zhuyin; the MOE manual is specifically a Zhuyin notation standard/reference; the NTU course explicitly pairs Zhuyin and Pinyin in pronunciation instruction. Preserve these different purposes rather than treating one as a substitute for the others.
- ACCC's series-level CEFR statement, Vol. 5's approximate B2–C1 claim, and FICHET's TBCL Level 4 label use different descriptions. Do not convert among them or map them to project levels without independent evidence.
- The MOE manual's webpage gives January 2017 for the digital edition, while the supplied EPUB metadata carries a 2016-12-31 date. Both dates are recorded with their different provenance.
- The MOE manual is CC BY-ND 3.0 Taiwan; its separately downloadable symbol-animation/audio assets are identified as CC BY 4.0 International. Other inspected web/PDF sources do not show a reuse license in the checked material; public access/download is not permission to republish.
- The two *Worshiping…* PDFs have identical SHA-256 hashes. Keep both local filenames; count one unique work.
- The local video is not included as evidence for pronunciation until creator, content, and accuracy are checked. The six audio folders contain files but no scripts; the apparent link to ACCC volumes is provisional.

## Review of collection sufficiency

- **Independent source families:** 21 works/material entries belong to seven identifiable families: ACCC; Fulbright/FLTA; Innovative Language Learning/ChineseClass101; MOE; NTU; FICHET/TMERC; and NAER. The six ACCC print books are one family, and the two FICHET products and two NAER resources are each grouped as one. Audio attribution is tentative; the video's family is unidentified.
- **Reliable coverage by level:** Not established. The collection has several traceable institutional sources, but no reliable source maps its material to all five project levels. The collection-wide source-family count is not evidence that each level is complete.
- **Six sections:** Some evidence exists for each section across the collection. Pronunciation references and Taiwan daily-life/culture topics are relatively well represented; vocabulary and grammar are broad but unlevelled; reading/writing and listening have significant progression, transcript, and feedback gaps.
- **Listening:** Huayu101 has phrase audio; FICHET's *驚豔臺灣學華語* links 12 MP3s and Chinese episode text/worksheets. The 734 local MP3 tracks are indexed in [audio-track-index.md](audio-track-index.md), but not listened to or transcribed; their connection to the books remains provisional. Identifiable, levelled listening evidence is insufficient.
- **Taiwan Mandarin suitability:** ACCC, Huayu101, and FICHET provide Taiwan-specific contexts; MOE and NTU pronunciation sources address standard Mandarin sounds. The collection still needs sourced Taiwanese register/usage distinctions across project levels; general Mandarin pronunciation content alone does not establish a Taiwan-specific curriculum.
- **Burmese:** No reviewed source provides Burmese explanations or translations.

- **Source count/families:** 21 distinct works/material entries and 7 identifiable source families; the six ACCC print volumes are one family. The six audio folders are provisionally associated with ACCC and do not count as six families. One video has no identified family.
- **Bibliographic traceability:** New cards ACCC-07–21 record available local identifiers, URLs, organizations, exact pages/chapters, publication-date uncertainty, rights, and limits. Missing authorship/date/license details are explicitly marked rather than inferred.
- **Duplicate check:** SHA-256 matched for the two *Worshiping…* PDFs. No file was deleted.
- **Coverage gate:** **NOT PASSED.** The detailed matrix records 0 Strong, 30 Partial, and 0 Missing cells. Every cell has some cross-level material, but none has adequate evidence assigned to its specific project level; Burmese explanations are also absent.
- **Audio inventory:** `audio-track-index.md` lists all 734 local MP3 filenames. 728 filename prefixes provisionally identify numbered lesson groups; six `00.mp3` files have no lesson number. No audio content or local transcript was verified.
- **Source review:** Online pages for MOE, NTU, Huayu101, *驚豔臺灣學華語*, and the NAER TBCL overview were accessible. The NAER first-level prototype PDF was inspected (pp. 1–8); its greeting dialogue, vocabulary tables, name example, and activity are recorded in ACCC-21. The linked *驚豔臺灣* audio folder listed all 12 MP3s and one linked PDF responded with PDF content. Local EPUB metadata/contents and sampled local PDF content were checked. The video was only inspected for local metadata and duration; its audiovisual content was not reviewed.
- **Reuse checks:** MOE license terms recorded; other license conditions not found on the inspected materials/pages are not treated as open licenses.
- **No Wiki lessons or HTML slides were created.**

**Conclusion:** The official and university resources substantially strengthen general pronunciation references, practical scenarios, and Taiwan-culture coverage. The collection has seven identifiable source families, but it does not yet evidence the full five-level, six-section Wiki curriculum. The NAER sources strengthen beginner-content context without validating the complete seven-expression list as Wiki A1. Keep the coverage gate blocked until level assignments and Burmese support are addressed. See [evidence-gaps.md](evidence-gaps.md) for source needs.

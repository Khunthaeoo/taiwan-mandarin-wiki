# Evidence gaps and source needs

This list is based on the source register, ACCC-01–ACCC-21, and the 30-cell matrix in [level-coverage-matrix.md](level-coverage-matrix.md). Source-family count is not volume count: the collection has **21 works/material entries and seven identifiable source families**. The six ACCC print volumes are one family; both FICHET resources and both NAER resources are each grouped as one family. The six audio folders are tentatively linked to ACCC, and the video family is unknown.

## 1. A1–A2 beginner vocabulary and grammar

**Current evidence:** ACCC-01–02 contain practical topics and grammar highlights, but neither volume is assigned to A1 or A2. ACCC-14 has survival phrases and grammar notes (lessons 1–3, pp. 3–15; contents p. 125) but no formal level or named author. ACCC-18 offers practical scenarios, with no level assignment. The NAER TBCL overview (ACCC-20) gives framework context, and its first-level prototype (ACCC-21) directly shows 你好 and related name material; the prototype does not directly support the full approved seven-expression list. TBCL Level 1 is below CEFR A1 in speaking and Levels 1–2 are below A1 in listening, reading, and writing, so those labels are not interchangeable with Wiki A1.

**Gap:** A project-level beginner vocabulary inventory, grammar progression, frequency/selection criteria, and reviewed exercises are absent. Burmese explanations are also absent.

**Seek:** Taiwan government or university-supported beginner curriculum materials that state their learner outcomes and lesson scope; a levelled vocabulary/grammar syllabus with traceable lesson/page references; and an independently reviewed Burmese–Traditional-Chinese glossary. Do not equate any source's level label with project A1/A2 without supporting evidence.

## 2. B1–B2 intermediate vocabulary and grammar

**Current evidence:** ACCC-03–05 provide broad thematic vocabulary and grammar; Vol. 5 reports 926 vocabulary items and 86 grammar points (ACCC-05, printed p. V) and describes its own difficulty as approximately CEFR B2–C1.

**Gap:** There is no validated B1/B2 boundary or project-level mapping. The CEFR claim is the book's characterization, not independent evidence that its lessons belong to project B2.

**Seek:** A university or government-supported intermediate syllabus with explicit objectives, grammar inventory, and vocabulary selection; independent sources for usage/register and Taiwan-specific vocabulary; and bridge/assessment materials with source-linked answer criteria.

## 3. C1 advanced vocabulary and grammar

**Current evidence:** ACCC-05–06 include debate topics, advanced grammar examples, reading, and written-expression tasks (e.g. ACCC-05, pp. V–VII and 6–7; ACCC-06, pp. 6–9). The materials do not assign individual content to project C1.

**Gap:** Advanced collocations, idioms, discourse/register, pragmatic nuance, and a complete C1 grammar/vocabulary scope are not established. Culturally or politically sensitive claims need corroboration.

**Seek:** University-level advanced Mandarin curricula, graded authentic Taiwanese texts with provenance, reference grammar/corpus evidence for contemporary Taiwan usage, and advanced writing/discussion assessment rubrics. Record actual level claims and do not infer C1 solely from difficulty.

## 4. Systematic listening materials with transcripts

**Current evidence:** Huayu101 exposes phrase-level audio controls and text/Pinyin (ACCC-18); FICHET *驚豔臺灣學華語* links 12 MP3s and reports Chinese episode text and Chinese/English worksheets (ACCC-19). The six local folders contain 734 MP3s but were not played or transcribed; their connection to ACCC is provisional. The separate ACCC listening workbooks were not supplied.

**Gap:** Most local tracks lack verified title, speaker, content, transcript, lesson alignment, level, and source attribution. The available official audio is useful but not a five-level listening sequence.

**Seek:** Publisher/official companion-audio pages, matching scripts/workbooks, episode metadata and lawful access terms; then verify track-to-lesson correspondence. Add levelled Taiwan Mandarin listening sets with full transcripts, timestamps, speakers/variety, comprehension tasks, and answer keys.

## 5. Reading and writing progression

**Current evidence:** ACCC-03 introduces beginning written language/discourse; Vols. 4–6 emphasize written language; Vol. 5 describes argument/paragraph practice; Vol. 6 includes short composition and written responses. The Volume 1–2 character-practice book, workbooks, and teacher materials are not supplied (ACCC-01–06).

**Gap:** A traceable staged path from character recognition and beginner writing through advanced genres, with feedback and answer support, is missing. MOE's Zhuyin character-annotation reference is not a Chinese composition curriculum.

**Seek:** Official or university-published graded readers and character-practice materials; writing prompts and model/rubric sets across levels; teacher-reviewed answer support; and explicit evidence for progression in Traditional Chinese.

## 6. Taiwan Mandarin pronunciation and tone-change practice

**Current evidence:** MOE's manual provides Zhuyin symbols, tone marks, and syllable tables (ACCC-16, pp. 4–7, 26–32); NTU's course outlines articulation, Zhuyin/Pinyin, four tones/light tone, tone changes, and polyphonic characters (ACCC-17, CH1–CH5). ACCC introductions discuss Pinyin and tones. The local video is unverified.

**Gap:** There is no mapped beginner-to-advanced sequence with listening discrimination, production feedback, and verified Taiwan Mandarin examples. A systematic, authoritative Hanyu Pinyin reference is still desirable. The Zhuyin manual is a reference, not a graduated exercise course.

**Seek:** MOE or university phonetics curricula and standards with practice material; Taiwan-focused recordings/examples for initials, finals, tones, neutral tone, tone sandhi, and prosody; source-backed Pinyin spelling conventions; and transparent instructor/recording attribution. Review the local MP4 only after identifying its creator and content.

## 7. Taiwan daily-life and cultural situations

**Current evidence:** ACCC-01–04, Huayu101, the FLTA *拜月老* reader, and FICHET *驚豔臺灣學華語* cover daily scenarios, food, transport, education, culture, festivals, and public topics.

**Gap:** Coverage is uneven by level and context. Practical, current service language/register and advanced social situations are incomplete. Some cultural narratives or public-issue statements are secondary, generalized, or time-sensitive.

**Seek:** Current Taiwan government sources for transport, health, housing, education, and public services; university/peer-reviewed sources for cultural and social context; and materials representing regional, generational, Indigenous, and other community perspectives. Preserve disagreements and cite precise original sections.

## 8. Burmese explanations and translations

**Current evidence:** None of the reviewed cards ACCC-01–19 contains Burmese instructional explanations.

**Gap:** Burmese glosses, grammar explanations, pronunciation guidance, and translation review are missing across all six sections and five levels.

**Seek:** A qualified Taiwan Mandarin/Burmese bilingual translator and a native Burmese language educator to produce and independently review original explanations. Establish a controlled terminology list, confirm natural Burmese usage, and retain source links for Mandarin claims. Do not machine-fill or fabricate Burmese translations.

## Review of collection sufficiency

- **Independent source families:** Six identifiable families is not the same as 19 works or 19 independent sources. Five are institutional/traceable for language-learning use (ACCC, Fulbright/FLTA, MOE, NTU, FICHET); ChineseClass101 is a commercial supplement. The video has no identified family, and the local audio attribution is provisional.
- **Reliable sources by level:** Not sufficient to demonstrate any complete project level. No family supplies evidence assignments for all level/section cells.
- **Six sections:** Each section has some source material somewhere in the collection; pronunciation references and Taiwan daily-life/culture topics are relatively well represented. Level-specific coverage remains partial throughout.
- **Listening:** Some identifiable phrase/podcast audio exists, but verified, levelled audio with matching transcripts is insufficient. None of the 734 local tracks has verified content in this audit.
- **Taiwan Mandarin:** Taiwan-focused textbooks and official resources are present, but contemporary colloquial/register variation is not established throughout A1–C1. General Mandarin pronunciation references should not be mistaken for a full Taiwan-specific curriculum.
